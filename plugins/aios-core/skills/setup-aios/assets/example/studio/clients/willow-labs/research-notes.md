# Willow Labs research notes

Six owner interviews inform the make-up credit decision. These summaries preserve the relevant observations without participant names or direct quotations.

## Sources

Mina supplied notes for S1–S4 on October 2. Alex conducted S5 and S6 on October 6. The summaries below are the evidence available for the readout.

Alex has no recordings or transcripts for S1–S4. Their reported times are owner estimates, not measurements. S5 and S6 add detail but do not independently verify the earlier accounts.

## Observations

| Participant | Source | Recent behavior | What it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Mina's September 28 interview | Reconciled credit balances across email and a spreadsheet before opening a new class series | Fragmented records create reconciliation work |
| S2 | Mina's September 28 interview | Estimated 45 minutes checking credit balances during a busy week | The burden can be noticeable, but the time is unmeasured |
| S3 | Mina's September 29 interview | Asked an instructor to confirm whether a make-up session had been used | Staff handoffs can leave uncertain balances |
| S4 | Mina's September 30 interview | Kept expired credits active after a customer dispute | Policy exceptions complicate a simple expiry rule |
| S5 | Alex's October 6 interview | Used a shared spreadsheet with one owner; reported no recent balance disputes | A simple existing process can be sufficient |
| S6 | Alex's October 6 interview | Offered fixed courses without make-up credits | The proposed problem does not apply to every studio |

S1 and S3 said they would consider a manual trial. Alex has not sought trial consent or agreed on dates.

## Synthesis

The recurring problem is deciding which credit balance to trust before a class series begins. It appears in four accounts, each with different rules and records.

The evidence supports testing reconciliation with studios that already experience this problem. It does not support replacing their booking software or targeting every ceramics studio.

S5 is useful counterevidence. A shared spreadsheet with clear ownership may solve the problem without a new product.

## Unresolved questions

- How often does reconciliation happen outside the busiest weeks?
- Does a shared record reduce time, or merely shift entry work to another person?
- Can an owner explain exceptions without extensive support from Mina?
- Would an owner continue using the process without someone running the trial?

No interview tested a price. Do not convert interest, frustration, or an estimated time saving into a revenue forecast.

## Use in the decision

The [readout](readout.md) proposes a manual trial to answer the first two questions. The [October 7 note](../../journal/2026-10-07-client-interviews.md) records how the additional interviews changed Alex's recommendation.
